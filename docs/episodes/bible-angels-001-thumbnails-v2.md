## A — recomendación principal · NO WINGS?

> Create a highly clickable, original 16:9 YouTube thumbnail for “Why Biblical Angels Look So Different”. Use reference image 1 for the exact identity of our recurring character Eli: white round face, thick eyebrows, dark beard and plain ochre cap. Use reference image 2 as the visual benchmark for the illustrated drawing style, exaggerated expressions, confident black outlines, flat colours and bold thumbnail typography. Show a huge close-up of Eli on the left with wide surprised eyes and a questioning hand gesture, looking at an ordinary-looking ancient traveller on the right. The traveller has a distinct face, simple linen tunic, mantle, sandals and a wooden staff; NO visible wings, halo or magical glow. Make the contrast between “ordinary person” and “unexpected angelic messenger” instantly readable. Deep saturated teal background, warm ochre clothing, clear silhouettes, minimal ancient-city detail. Include the exact words “NO WINGS?” in enormous bright yellow uppercase lettering with a thick black outline across the top, comfortably inside safe margins, not covering either face. One visual question, two clear subjects, strong separation and mobile readability. Hand-drawn 2D educational Bible cartoon. No photorealism, CGI, horror, extra text, watermarks or logos.

## B — contraste y seis alas contables · SIX WINGS?

> Create a dramatic, highly readable 16:9 YouTube thumbnail for an illustrated episode about biblical descriptions of angels. Use reference image 1 to preserve Eli’s exact face, beard and plain ochre cap. Use reference image 2 for the bold hand-drawn cartoon style, expressive faces, thick dark outlines, flat colours and attention-grabbing typography. Place one large simplified seraph on the left, with EXACTLY SIX clearly countable wings arranged in three distinct pairs: two upper wings covering its face, two lower wings covering its feet, and two middle wings extended for flight, as described in Isaiah 6:2. Separate the silhouettes so all six can be counted. On the right, show Eli in a large close-up with eyebrows raised, eyes focused on the wings and one surprised pointing gesture. Saturated deep indigo background, cream and warm ochre wings, clean composition and strong contrast. Include the exact words “SIX WINGS?” in huge bright yellow uppercase lettering with a thick black outline along the top; keep lettering away from faces and wing tips. No four faces, eye-covered wheels, swords, extra wings, feather clutter, photorealism, CGI, horror, watermarks, logos or additional words. This is an educational illustration of Isaiah’s vision, not a photograph.

## C — objeto extraño y curiosidad · FULL OF EYES

> Create a striking, highly clickable 16:9 YouTube thumbnail about the eye-covered wheels in Ezekiel’s vision. Use reference image 1 for Eli’s exact recognizable character identity, and reference image 2 for the hand-drawn illustration style, exaggerated but clear expressions, thick black contours, flat colours and powerful thumbnail typography. Make one large nested-wheel motif the dominant object in the centre-right: two intersecting wheel rings, with distinct stylized eyes distributed around the rims. Clearly draw the eyes, black pupils and thick outlines so the unusual feature reads instantly on a phone. In the lower-left foreground, show a large close-up of Eli, with a startled, curious expression, looking toward the wheel. Deep saturated burgundy background, warm ochre wheel, cream eyes and clean separation. Include the exact words “FULL OF EYES” in very large bright yellow uppercase lettering with a thick black outline at the top, inside safe margins. Keep one dominant curiosity object and one reaction face. Educational biblical doodle illustration, simple background, no photorealism, CGI, spaceships, aliens, mechanical engines, gore, horror, watermarks, logos or other text. Present it as an artistic interpretation of a prophetic vision.

Recomendación editorial inicial: A es la combinación más clara con el título general; B es una alternativa de mayor riesgo anatómico; C puede destacar por la rareza del objeto. No afirmar que alguno es el ganador medido antes de probarlo con audiencia. No usar “every angel” como promesa exhaustiva: el guion analiza mensajeros, figuras nombradas y visiones seleccionadas, no todas las tradiciones o menciones posibles.
